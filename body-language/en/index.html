WHY THIS EXISTS

Training is individual. Awareness makes that visible.

Two people can do the same exercise and experience it differently. BODY LANGUAGE creates time to notice those differences before deciding what to focus on next.

Participants move through guided, adaptable sequences at their own pace. They observe what feels available, demanding or worth exploring, then identify three personal focus areas. These are prompts for further attention, not a diagnosis or a ready-made training plan.

INSIDE THE WORKSHOP

A simple journey from observation to focus.

01 / NOTICE

Explore movement

Guided movement and manageable effort invite observation across mobility, stability, strength, posture and movement patterns.

02 / CONNECT

Include the whole experience

Participants also notice breath, tension and recovery. They can adjust or skip an exercise and are never required to perform for the group.

03 / TAKE FORWARD

Name three focus areas

Each person privately records three areas they want to pay attention to and may discuss them with their own coach or trainer.

THE SEVEN LENSES

Mobility · Stability · Strength · Posture & movement patterns · Breath · Tension · Recovery

WHAT PEOPLE TAKE AWAY

Clarity they can carry into practice.

“What am I noticing? What would I like to understand better? Where should I direct my attention?”

The participant owns their notes. Sharing with a coach is their choice. The venue's trainers remain responsible for exercise technique, training plans and progression. The workshop helps people arrive at that conversation with more specific observations.

BEHIND THE FORMAT

Practical experience, clearly bounded.

Mel Mihira brings a physiotherapy foundation, functional anatomy, structural bodywork and yoga training to her work with movement and body awareness. Her practice has developed across Germany, North India, Dubai and Sri Lanka, including multidisciplinary clinical work in India.

BODY LANGUAGE translates that experience into a structured group workshop. It supports personal observation and conversation with existing coaches; it is not a medical assessment, treatment or promise of athletic improvement.

GOOD TO KNOW

Before you begin.

Do participants need training experience?

No. The exercises can be adapted or skipped. We clarify the audience and practical setting with the host in advance.

Is this therapy or a performance test?

No. We do not diagnose, treat injuries, assess suitability for a sport or prescribe training. Participants with pain or health concerns should seek advice from an appropriately qualified clinician.

Will the venue receive personal notes?

No. Each participant keeps their own notes and decides whether to share any observation with a trainer.

How much does it cost?

Hosts receive a written quote after we agree group size, location, duration and delivery costs. A venue sets its own participant price and booking process.
